Decoding the Problem Statement
Strip away the wording and the brief is asking for two connected products, not one:
• Traveller side: a way to find local experiences that actually fit a person's real, specific situation right now —
not a generic ranked list.
• Provider side: a way for small local businesses, guides, and artists to be found by the travellers who
genuinely want what they offer, even though they can't afford marketing or SEO.

live-adaptation problem, not a ranking problem. Say this explicitly in your pitch — it immediately signals you
understood the brief at a level most of the 150 teams will not.

05 The 12 Differentiators — This Is What Wins
Pick 3–4 of these to actually build well rather than attempting all 12 shallowly. Each entry below gives you the
pitch line, the mechanism, and — most importantly — the specific competitor gap it closes, so you can defend it
under judge questioning.
1 Constraint-Solver Recommendation Engine
One-line pitch: We don't rank experiences — we pack a feasible plan that actually fits your time, budget, and distance
together.
How it works: Score every experience for interest-fit and quality, then run a lightweight scheduling pass (greedy or
backtracking) that only accepts a combination if total time + travel time + total cost stays inside the traveller's stated limits.
Why competitors don't do this: Competitors personalise the ranking of single items. None of them check whether
several picks can actually be done together in the time and money available.
Build effort: Medium
2 Live Re-Planning Engine ("Plan B, Instantly")
One-line pitch: One tap regenerates your plan the moment reality changes — rain, a closed venue, less time, less
money.
How it works: Re-run the same constraint solver with one updated variable (time remaining, budget remaining, or an
"unavailable" flag on a venue) and instantly return a new feasible set.
Why competitors don't do this: This directly attacks the exact gap GetYourGuide's own 2026 research surfaced: AI
helps people plan, nobody helps them adapt live.
Build effort: Medium
3 "Why This For You" Explainability Layer
One-line pitch: Every suggestion comes with one honest sentence on why it was picked for you specifically.
How it works: Reuse the same scoring signals (distance, budget fit, interest match, rating) to auto-fill a template: "Picked
because: fits your 2 hrs, 400m away, top-rated for families, within budget."
Why competitors don't do this: GetYourGuide only summarises reviews; nobody explains the personalisation match
itself. This builds trust fast and costs very little to build.
Build effort: Easy
4 Micro-Moment / Time-Boxed Discovery Mode
One-line pitch: A dedicated "I have exactly ___ hours" mode — straight from the brief's own family example.
How it works: A single prominent time-slider that filters and packs only experiences (plus travel buffer) that can
realistically be finished inside the stated window.
Why competitors don't do this: Every competitor treats duration as a minor filter buried in an advanced-filters panel, not
a first-class entry point.
Build effort: Easy–Medium

5 Group Consensus Planner
One-line pitch: Everyone in the group taps their own interests; we find the experience that keeps the whole group happy
— and respects everyone's accessibility needs.
How it works: Each member submits a mini interest poll; the engine maximises joint interest overlap while treating any
one member's accessibility requirement as a hard constraint for the whole group.
Why competitors don't do this: No researched competitor offers group-vote based experience matching today — trip
decisions are still made by one person searching alone.
Build effort: Medium
6 Hidden-Gem Signal Mining
One-line pitch: Surfacing the good local spots that never had the marketing budget to be "listed" anywhere.
How it works: Let local contributors submit unlisted spots with a lightweight "verified by local" badge flow, alongside
registered provider listings.
Why competitors don't do this: Catalogue platforms are pay-to-list by design, so the smallest, most authentic vendors
stay invisible — directly named in the brief as "hidden places."
Build effort: Medium (mockable for a demo)
7 Provider AI Co-Pilot for Listing Creation
One-line pitch: A business owner types two lines in plain language; we generate the full, structured listing.
How it works: An LLM prompt turns a one-line description into a title, description, category tags, accessibility flags, and a
suggested price band based on similar local listings.
Why competitors don't do this: Existing operator tools (e.g. GetYourGuide's) offer dashboards and reply-suggestions —
none generate a full listing from scratch for a non-technical small owner.
Build effort: Easy–Medium
8 Demand-Aware Provider Matching (push, not pull)
One-line pitch: Providers get alerted: "3 families near you want a 2-hour food experience under Rs. 500 in the next 3
hours."
How it works: A simple matching job compares live, anonymised traveller queries against provider offerings and
geo-radius, then pushes a notification to matching providers.
Why competitors don't do this: This flips the entire model from "traveller must search" to "provider gets found" — the
exact problem the brief raises about small businesses being invisible.
Build effort: Medium
9 Accessibility-First Filtering as a Core Layer
One-line pitch: Accessibility isn't a checkbox we bury in settings — it's a hard constraint the solver can never violate.
How it works: Structured accessibility tags (wheelchair access, sensory-friendly, dietary, elderly-friendly) live on every
listing and are enforced the same way budget and time are enforced.
Why competitors don't do this: Explicitly named in the brief, yet treated as a minor filter (if present at all) across every
platform reviewed.
Build effort: Easy

10 Existing-Itinerary-Aware Gap Filling
One-line pitch: Paste your existing plan; we find the free gaps and fill them with things you can actually reach in time.
How it works: Parse a typed or pasted itinerary into time blocks, detect free gaps, and run the same solver scoped to
only that gap's time and location.
Why competitors don't do this: Every competitor generates a plan from a blank slate — none of them attach to a plan
the traveller already has, despite the brief naming "existing itinerary" directly.
Build effort: Medium
11 Sustainability / Local-Impact Score
One-line pitch: A simple badge showing how much of your spend actually stays with the local community.
How it works: A transparent heuristic score based on business size/ownership type, shown as a small badge on each
listing.
Why competitors don't do this: Ties directly into the brief's framing of connecting travellers to genuinely local
businesses — a framing no mainstream competitor scores or surfaces today.
Build effort: Easy
12 Conversational + Voice Multimodal Entry
One-line pitch: "I've got 3 hours before my flight, want something fun and cheap near the airport" — said or typed —
becomes a real, feasible plan.
How it works: An LLM parses free text or voice into the same structured constraints (time, budget, location, interests) that
feed directly into the solver.
Why competitors don't do this: The industry is already moving here (GetYourGuide's ChatGPT app, TripAdvisor's AI
Assistant) — but they hand back generic text. You close the loop into an actual constraint-checked plan.
Build effort: Easy–Medium

07 The 5-Minute Demo Script
A clear story beats a feature tour. Use one persona and walk judges through it step by step.
1. Set the scene (20 sec)
"Meet the Sharma family — 2 hours before their flight, near Bandra, a Rs. 1,500 budget, and one member uses
a wheelchair."
2. Show the entry point (30 sec)
Type or speak the request in one natural sentence; watch it turn into structured constraints on screen.
3. Show the solved plan (45 sec)
Reveal a feasible 2-hour plan — two experiences that fit the time, travel time, budget, and accessibility need —
each with its "why this" tag.
4. Trigger a disruption (30 sec)
Tap "It just started raining." Watch the plan instantly rebuild around an indoor alternative. This is your most
memorable moment — do not rush it.
5. Switch to the provider view (45 sec)
Show a small business owner typing one line; the AI co-pilot generates a full listing in seconds. Then show a live
demand-alert: "2 families nearby are searching for what you offer."
6. Close on impact (20 sec)
End on the local-impact score badge and one closing line: "We don't just recommend experiences — we
guarantee they fit your real life, and we adapt the moment life changes."

08 Hackathon Judging: How To Optimise Your Pitch
• Open with the reframe, not the feature list. In the first 20 seconds, say plainly that this is a
constraint-solving and live-adaptation problem, not a search problem. It signals depth immediately.
• Quote the real 2026 statistic. "69% of travellers already use AI to plan a trip, but only 17% use it to book an
activity" — a researched number lands far better than a claimed insight.
• Lead your live demo with the disruption moment. The re-plan-on-rain moment is the single most
differentiating thing you can show — don't bury it at the end.
• Never skip the provider side. Many of the 150 teams will forget it entirely; even a basic, working provider
co-pilot demo covers a full third of the brief and stands out by default.
• Don't over-promise "AI." A transparent rule-based engine that visibly works beats a black-box model that
breaks on stage.
• Keep the UI simple and mobile-first. Judges see dozens of demos; clarity reads as competence.
• Memorise one closing line. Something like: "We guarantee your plan still works when your real day doesn't
go as planned."

10 Glossary — Key Terms, Explained Simply
Constraint solver
Logic that finds options which satisfy several rules at once (time, money, distance) together — not one rule at a
time.
Isochrone
A map zone showing everywhere reachable within a set number of minutes — smarter than a plain circle radius.
Explainability
Showing the user, in one plain sentence, WHY something was recommended to them specifically.
Demand-alert
A push notification to a business saying people who want exactly what they offer are nearby right now.
Hidden-gem mining
Finding good local spots that aren't on any official, paid listing.
Local-impact score
A badge showing how much of a traveller's spend stays with the local community versus a large chain.
Feasibility packing
Checking that a combination of chosen experiences can actually be done together inside the available time,
travel time, and budget.
